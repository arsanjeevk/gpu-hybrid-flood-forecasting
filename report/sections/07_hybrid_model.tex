\section{Hybrid Physics--AI Model}
\label{sec:hybrid-model}

\subsection{Residual-learning dataset}

ANUGA and JAX fields were aligned on their common 50.0~m grid and matching
60.0~s output times.  For each transition from \(t\) to \(t+1\), the target
was the physical-unit residual
\[
  \mathbf{r}_{t+1}
  = \mathbf{q}^{\mathrm{ANUGA}}_{t+1}
  - \mathbf{q}^{\mathrm{JAX}}_{t+1},
  \qquad
  \mathbf{q}=(h,u,v).
\]
The six input channels were JAX depth, \(x\)-velocity, and \(y\)-velocity at
time \(t\), rainfall at \(t\), terrain, and Manning roughness.  Dynamic and
static channels were normalised using statistics recorded with the dataset;
the residual remained in physical units at the network output.

The 60 transitions were divided into contiguous chronological blocks of 42
training, 9 validation, and 9 test samples, corresponding to configured
fractions of 0.70 and 0.15 before integer allocation.  Timesteps were not
randomly shuffled because adjacent flood states are strongly autocorrelated
and would otherwise cross the evaluation boundary.  The loss mask was derived
only from the maximum ANUGA depth in the training block, using the configured
\(10^{-4}\)~m threshold.  It retained 171,619 of the 172,841 common-domain
cells.  This procedure reduces direct temporal leakage, but the test block
still belongs to the same simulated rainfall event and is not independent
event validation.

\subsection{Network and objective}

The residual predictor is a Flax encoder--decoder with U-Net-style skip
connections~\cite{RonnebergerEtAl2015}.  The configured depth was three, with
16, 32, and 64 channels, \(3\times3\) convolutions, GELU activations, and
three output channels.  Stride-two convolutions perform downsampling;
transposed convolutions perform upsampling; and a zero-initialised
\(1\times1\) convolution produces the residual.  Training used
128 by 128 spatial patches, four sampled patches per training timestep, and a
batch size of four.

For target residual \(\mathbf r\), prediction \(\widehat{\mathbf r}\), and
training residual standard deviations \(\mathbf s\), the data terms were
computed on the activity mask as
\[
  L_{\mathrm{data}}
  = 1.0\,\operatorname{MSE}
    \left((\widehat{\mathbf r}-\mathbf r)/\mathbf s\right)
  +0.1\,\operatorname{MAE}
    \left((\widehat{\mathbf r}-\mathbf r)/\mathbf s\right).
\]
An additional unit-weight penalty applied the squared rectified negative part
of the corrected depth, normalised by the depth residual scale.  Permanently
dry background cells were excluded from all three terms.

Optimisation used AdamW~\cite{LoshchilovHutter2019} with an initial learning
rate of 0.001, cosine decay, two warm-up epochs, weight decay
\(10^{-5}\), and gradient-norm clipping at 1.0.  Training was configured for
100 epochs with validation early stopping of patience 10 and minimum
improvement \(10^{-6}\).  The completed CPU run reached all 100 epochs; the
selected checkpoint was epoch 94 with a normalised validation objective of
0.855199.  This dimensionless training objective is not directly comparable
with physical-unit RMSE.

\subsection{Autoregressive coupling}

For every 60.0~s correction interval, the structured finite-volume solver
first advanced adaptively from the current corrected state.  The residual
network then evaluated features assembled from the state at the beginning of
that interval, consistent with the one-step training semantics.  Residuals
were clipped independently at five training standard deviations and applied
with a validation-selected relaxation factor of 0.05.

Depth was clipped to non-negative values.  The provisional corrected depth
field was then scaled over the active domain to preserve the total water
volume produced by that physics interval.  Thus, the neural model could
redistribute depth but could not introduce an unmodelled net source or sink.
Corrected velocities were converted back to momentum, with zero momentum in
cells below the JAX dry threshold of \(10^{-4}\)~m.  Both the interval
substeps and the outer correction loop use \texttt{jax.lax.scan}; the full
rollout is JIT-compatible and does not require per-step host transfers.

The saved comparison product contains ANUGA, pure JAX, and hybrid fields on a
common \((\texttt{source},\texttt{time},\texttt{y},\texttt{x})\) grid.  The
relaxation parameter was selected on the chronological validation window, so
the held-out test-window results are reported separately in
Section~\ref{sec:results}.
